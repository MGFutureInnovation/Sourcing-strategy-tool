You can optimize for two, but rarely all three. Projects that try to maximize speed, differentiation, AND minimize investment typically stall in paralysis while competitors move forward with "good enough" solutions.

This tool helps you make deliberate choices about which constraints matter most for this specific project, then recommends an approach that aligns with your priorities. The output creates a consistent position from scoping through business case.

⚠️ Note: This tool is designed for product development decisions in established categories. For R&D opportunities or moonshot concepts, consult Future Innovation Lead.

What sub-category is this project for?
Different products have different dynamics - this helps tailor the advice.
What's the primary goal of this project?
Pick what THIS project is really about - your goal shapes which approach makes sense.
What's your volume expectation?
Higher volumes justify bigger investments. Use realistic year 2-3 estimates, not optimistic forecasts.
What's your timeline to market?
Faster timelines limit your options. Consider what retail windows and competition actually require.
What's your current position in this specific sub-category?
Leaders can afford to invest and wait. Challengers and new entrants often need scrappier approaches.
How crowded is this market space?
In crowded markets, you need to stand out. In open markets, moving fast to establish position matters more.
Do you have in-house capability to develop this?
Can your team actually build this? Gaps in expertise push toward partnerships or sourcing.
What's the IP situation in this space?
This determines your freedom to act. Existing IP relationships constrain which approaches are realistic.
What level of product customization do you need?
More customization = more time and cost. Only go deep if differentiation truly demands it.
What's your investment appetite for this project?
Match spend to strategic importance. Not every project needs to be a big bet.
